About

A free school for how money works.

Solar Financial explains money, debt, stocks, artificial intelligence and risk — how they work, why they matter and how they shape the world. It is free, it gives no investment advice, and every lesson is signed by the people who wrote it.

Why it exists

Financial decisions shape almost every life, yet the explanations of how the system works are usually written for people who already know. Solar Financial starts from the opposite assumption: that a curious reader, given clear language, good examples and honest sources, can understand how money is created, why a bond price moves, what a share is worth and where the profits from a new technology end up.

The front page is laid out like a newspaper because that is how most people meet financial news. The school behind it is organised into five desks — Money, Debt, Stocks, AI & Emerging Markets, and Risk — plus Case Files, a series of historical investigations into the moments when money broke.

It is the fifth instrument of Sol Invictus, alongside its other instruments, and shares their frame: one question at a time, every fact sourced, every judgement signed.

Editorial standards

The rules every lesson and case file follows.

Sourced

Every financial and historical figure is checked against a primary or institutional source — central banks, statistical agencies, company filings, original documents — and listed beneath the piece. We do not invent quotations, figures or references.

Signed

Every piece carries the name of its author. First drafts may be prepared with AI assistance; both founders then review the text and check its claims before the draft notice is removed.

Separated

We keep facts, analysis and opinion apart, and say which is which. Where the evidence is uncertain or contested, we say that too — and we distinguish an opportunity from a demonstrated outcome.

How a lesson works

Every lesson has one central question, takes about five minutes to read, contains one chart with its data and source, and ends with one takeaway. Technical words are explained where they first appear. Case Files are longer and more archival: a timeline, the economic background, the mechanism, the evidence, the people involved, the consequences and the sources.

On every chart and figure, green marks a rise and red a fall; the sign and an arrow always say the same thing, so colour is never the only signal. Market data in the strip on the front page comes from the European Central Bank's daily reference rates, labelled with their date. When that data cannot be loaded, the strip shows the contents of the edition instead — never invented figures. The streams of numbers behind the front-page emblem are decoration, not data.
