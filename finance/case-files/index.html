15 August 1971

1971: the night the dollar left gold

Under the Bretton Woods system, foreign governments could convert their dollars into US gold at $35 an ounce. As dollars accumulated abroad faster than gold in Fort Knox, the promise became impossible to keep for everyone at once. President Nixon suspended convertibility in August 1971. An attempt to rebuild fixed exchange rates at a new gold price failed, and by March 1973 the major currencies were floating — the system of fiat money and floating exchange rates that still governs the world economy.

By Matthieu Théard17 November 20266 min read
