Inter-Agency Standing CommitteeWho we are, and how the humanitarian system coordinates

The IASC is the primary global mechanism for inter-agency humanitarian coordination: a forum, not an operational agency, that brings UN and non-UN humanitarian organizations together to set policy, agree priorities and coordinate response.

What we do

Four functions, one coordinated system

The IASC itself does not run field operations. Its functions are about aligning the organizations that do.

  1. 01Formulate humanitarian policy and guidance
  2. 02Agree strategic priorities for the sector
  3. 03Coordinate collective response to crises
  4. 04Advance protection, accountability and principled action
03Humanitarian principles

The four principles that guide humanitarian action

These underpin every IASC function and every humanitarian operation coordinated through it.

Humanity

Human suffering must be addressed wherever it is found. The purpose of humanitarian action is to protect life and health and ensure respect for human beings.

Impartiality

Humanitarian action must be carried out on the basis of need alone, giving priority to the most urgent cases, without discrimination.

Neutrality

Humanitarian actors must not take sides in hostilities or engage in controversies of a political, racial, religious or ideological nature.

Independence

Humanitarian action must remain autonomous from the political, economic, military or other objectives that any actor may hold.

05The Secretariat

Keeping the system connected

The Secretariat does not set policy itself; it makes sure the Principals' decisions are followed through.

The IASC Secretariat supports the IASC Principals in their deliberations, helps ensure their decisions are acted upon, and builds connectivity and synergies between the Principals, the Emergency Directors Group, the Operational Policy and Advocacy Group, the Results Groups and the wider network of entities associated with the IASC.
