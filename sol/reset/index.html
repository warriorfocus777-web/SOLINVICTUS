01Define

Response is concentrating where needs are most severe

Prioritization and planning have become more focused, with some reduction in the time required for collective planning.

Read with care

“A smaller appeal footprint is a change in planning arrangements, rather than evidence of reduced needs.” Eight operations have planned transitions, five already accelerated; 20 country plans were reprioritized in April 2025.

02Deliver

Coordinated action reached 66 million of the 148 million people targeted

Coordination arrangements are being streamlined, pooled funding has expanded and reported shares of cash assistance have increased.

Also delivered

  • $303Mallocated through CERF's Rapid Response Window in 2026, plus $9M through the Underfunded Emergencies Window.
  • 5pilot countries integrating supply chains under UN80 reforms: Afghanistan, Haiti, the Occupied Palestinian Territory, Somalia and Sudan.
  • $10MCERF envelope for disability and inclusion.
  • AIFramework for the Responsible Use of AI in Humanitarian Action, for HWG endorsement in October 2026.

Read with care

“Operational contributions depend on award conditions, timely disbursement and access for the better placed actors.” CVA targets of 35% and 85% are envisaged; endorsement status and operational application will be clarified through HWG review.

03Devolve

Local participation is broadening; access to resources and decisions has further to go

Steps are being taken to broaden local actors' participation and strengthen country-level accountability mechanisms, with further work needed on access to resources and decision-making.

Perception and representation

  • 56%of Perception Survey respondents see coordination as more inclusive of local actors.Preliminary, September 2026
  • 26%consider financial, operational and compliance risks equitably shared.
  • ~½of operations reported at least 15% local and national representation in HCTs, 2024 and 2025.

Read with care

Perception Survey results “reflect respondents' perceptions and cannot be treated as representative of the wider humanitarian community; delayed country responses may also change the findings.” A mutual accountability mechanism has been piloted and will be institutionalized in 2027.

04Defend

The IASC has stood visibly with frontline colleagues under sustained pressure on norms

Collective advocacy and humanitarian diplomacy have continued, alongside efforts to uphold protection, gender equality and PSEAH commitments.

“In highly politicized crises, including Sudan, OPT, DRC and Ukraine, the IASC has stood visibly and consistently with frontline colleagues.”

What collective advocacy covers

  • IHLPrincipled humanitarian action and consistent application of IHL and International Human Rights Law, by the ERC, Principals and HCs/HCTs.
  • GBVThe life-saving nature of gender-based violence response and risk mitigation.
  • PSEAHKept distinct from, and complementary to, efforts on conflict-related sexual violence.

Next engagement

27October 2026
IASC Principals – Donor Engagement

Focus on complementarity, quality financing and proportionate approaches to risk and due diligence, to agree concrete donor and IASC actions with measurable follow-up.

Read with care

The Stocktake reports Defend progress qualitatively; it gives no quantitative indicators, so none are charted. Further work will document implementation of the Principals' humanitarian diplomacy recommendation.

Next phase

Turning implementation steps into more consistent improvements in assistance and protection

“With constrained resources, strained trust and a fragmented political environment, there is a real risk of losing core humanitarian capacities, leaving more needs unmet and reducing preparedness for escalating crises.”
