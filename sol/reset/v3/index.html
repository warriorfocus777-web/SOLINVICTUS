Humanitarian Reset · Stocktake

Humanitarian Reset Stocktake, October 2026 · draft for discussion

A humanitarian system fit for the realities of today.

The Humanitarian Reset brings the humanitarian community together around four imperatives: prioritize the most urgent needs, deliver more effectively, move resources and decision-making closer to affected communities, and defend the principles that make humanitarian action possible.

Four imperatives. One collective responsibility. A system measured by the difference it makes.

Three people counts

100 million, 66 million and 41 million are different measures

Do not add them together.

  • 100MCollective results. The Stocktake introduction reports collective results for 100 million people, “including 41 million with life-saving support”. The sources used for this page do not set out how the 100 million is counted or how it overlaps with people reached.

03 · The guiding principle

The Reset must be felt by the people it exists to serve.

Editorial paraphrase, not a quotation. It restates the people-first intent Tom Fletcher sets out in conversation with UNDP's Beyond Crisis series (opens in a new tab).

The test is the difference made in the lives of people affected by crises. By the Stocktake's own account, that test has not yet been applied:

“…the IASC is yet to assess outcomes of the Humanitarian Reset for the lives of vulnerable people.”

Stocktake, introduction

04The four imperatives

Define, Deliver, Devolve, Defend: four connected imperatives, not four steps

Each imperative depends on the others. Prioritization means little without delivery; delivery is not sustainable without local agency; none of it is possible without the principles that protect humanitarian space.

  • Define“…priorities that truly save lives”Focus the response where needs are most severe.Evidence for Define →
  • Deliver“…humanity and accountability”Turn collective capability into effective assistance.Evidence for Deliver →
  • Devolve“…local partners who know best”Put resources, decisions and agency closer to the frontlines.Evidence for Devolve →
  • Defend“…humanitarian law and principles”Protect the principles that make humanitarian action possible.Evidence for Defend →

01Define · prioritize what matters most

Response is concentrating where needs are most severe

What is changing: Prioritization and planning have become more focused, with some reduction in the time required for collective planning.

“…priorities that truly save lives”

Tom Fletcher, UNDP Beyond Crisis (opens in a new tab)

Strategic commitmentSharpen priorities around the most severe humanitarian needs, while staying alert to serious needs and protection risks outside the highest severity categories.

Evidence and indicators

What the evidence means

Prioritization is becoming more concentrated on people in the most severe locations. That does not mean serious needs in severity 3 locations have disappeared, and a smaller appeal footprint is not evidence that needs have fallen.

Read with care

  • “A smaller appeal footprint is a change in planning arrangements, rather than evidence of reduced needs.” (Stocktake)
  • The 62% (Stocktake) and 55% (presentation) values for 2026 are not yet reconciled.
  • Cluster plans (2024–2025) and HNRPs (2026) are separate measures.
  • Eight operations have planned transitions, five already accelerated; 20 country plans were reprioritized in April 2025.
Open questions, next steps and sources

What remains unresolved

How does the system prioritize the most severe needs without overlooking serious protection risks and needs in other locations?

Next steps

  • Continued prioritization (next phase).
  • HPC median of 160 days by end-2027 (target).
  • Reconcile the 2026 severity share across the two documents.

02Deliver · make collective action work

Coordinated action reached 66 million of the 148 million people targeted

What is changing: Coordination arrangements are being streamlined, pooled funding has expanded and reported shares of cash assistance have increased.

“…humanity and accountability”

Tom Fletcher, UNDP Beyond Crisis (opens in a new tab)

Strategic commitmentImprove the speed, quality, coordination and reach of humanitarian delivery while maintaining humanity and accountability.

Evidence and indicators

Also delivered or in train

  • $303Mallocated through CERF's Rapid Response Window in 2026, plus $9M through the Underfunded Emergencies Window.
  • 5pilot countries integrating supply chains under UN80 reforms: Afghanistan, Haiti, the Occupied Palestinian Territory, Somalia and Sudan.
  • $10MCERF envelope for disability and inclusion.
  • AIFramework for the Responsible Use of AI in Humanitarian Action, listed for HWG endorsement in October 2026. Pending verificationNo record of endorsement located as of 19 November 2026.

What the evidence means

The Stocktake documents reach, pooled-fund growth and coordination reform. These describe different dimensions of delivery and stay separate here. An allocation is not a disbursement, and a person reached is not in itself evidence of the quality or durability of assistance.

Read with care

  • “Operational contributions depend on award conditions, timely disbursement and access for the better placed actors.” (Stocktake)
  • 66M reached is 45% of 148M targeted; it is not the 100M collective-results figure.
  • CVA targets of 35% and 85% are envisaged; endorsement status and operational application will be clarified through HWG review.
Open questions, next steps and sources

What remains unresolved

Does a more streamlined system translate into faster, safer, better-quality assistance for people affected by crises?

Next steps

  • Confirm HWG endorsement status of the AI framework.
  • Clarify CVA targets through HWG review.
  • Simpler ways of working (next phase).

03Devolve · move power and resources closer

Local participation is broadening; access to resources and decisions has further to go

What is changing: Steps are being taken to broaden local actors' participation and strengthen country-level accountability mechanisms, with further work needed on access to resources and decision-making.

“…local partners who know best”

Tom Fletcher, UNDP Beyond Crisis (opens in a new tab)

Strategic commitmentStrengthen local and national actors' participation, access to resources, decision-making authority and accountability, while addressing inequitable transfer of risk.

Evidence and indicators

Perception and representation

  • 56%of Perception Survey respondents see coordination as more inclusive of local actors.Preliminary, September 2026 · respondents' perceptions, not representative
  • 26%consider financial, operational and compliance risks equitably shared.
  • ~½of operations reported at least 15% local and national representation in HCTs, 2024 and 2025.

What the evidence means

Localization is not only about who implements projects. It is about who controls resources, sets priorities, takes part in decisions, bears risk and answers to communities. Broader participation does not by itself establish equitable authority or financing: only 26% of survey respondents consider risk equitably shared.

Read with care

  • Perception Survey results “reflect respondents' perceptions and cannot be treated as representative of the wider humanitarian community; delayed country responses may also change the findings.” (Stocktake)
  • 55% and 20% are the same 2026 portfolio with different inclusion criteria; data as of 28 September, to be verified in H1 2027.
  • The 2026 women-led share runs to 11 September only.
  • Selected country fund shares are from different years and are not comparable across funds.
Open questions, next steps and sources

What remains unresolved

Are local and national organizations gaining genuine authority, predictable resources and equitable sharing of financial, operational and compliance risks?

Next steps

  • Verification of 2026 pooled-fund data, H1 2027.
  • Mutual accountability mechanism, piloted, to be institutionalized in 2027.
  • Lessons from the first US award applied to the second round.

  • Stocktake, Devolve (draft, 8 Oct 2026 rev.)

Why there is no Defend scorecard

The Stocktake reports Defend progress qualitatively and gives no quantitative indicators. Advocacy, diplomatic engagement and policy commitments are activities; whether they produce practical protection for civilians, principled access or safety for humanitarian personnel has not been measured. This page does not create a proxy number to fill that gap.

What the evidence means

Defend is a qualitative, principled pillar in the current Stocktake. The evidence shows sustained collective advocacy; it does not yet show its effect on protection and access outcomes.

Read with care

  • No quantitative indicators are reported, so none are charted.
  • PSEAH and conflict-related sexual violence are distinct areas of work.
Open questions, next steps and sources

What remains unresolved

How does collective advocacy translate into practical protection for civilians, principled access and safety for humanitarian personnel?

Next steps

  • Document implementation of the Principals' humanitarian diplomacy recommendation.
  • Confirm follow-up actions from the 27 October donor engagement.

06Complementarity · the connective principle

The right combination of actors and capabilities, working towards shared outcomes

Adopted June 2026 (presentation)

“Seeking complementarity means shifting the system from institutional competition to the right mix of incentives and discipline to ensure that the best mix of actors achieve shared collective outcomes.”

Source: Presentation, slide 14 (draft).

Complementarity lets different humanitarian actors contribute their distinct capabilities towards shared outcomes. It should reduce institutional competition and duplication while strengthening collective responsibility, country-level leadership and accountability to people affected by crises.

07Voices and frontline perspective

The perspective of people affected is the evidence still missing

This page carries no testimony from people affected by crises or from local responders, because none has been cleared for use. It does not substitute invented or illustrative voices.

What the Stocktake has

  • 52%of HNRP countries' HCTs regularly discuss community feedback trends, down from 65%.
  • 56%of Perception Survey respondents, who are coordination actors rather than affected people, see coordination as more inclusive of local actors.

What is missing

An assessment of outcomes for people affected by crises; the Stocktake states it has not yet been done. Community consultations are an ongoing workstream with no results reported yet.

Not assessed

How voices will be added

Only verified, cleared and attributed material, with documented consent, safeguarding and security review, date, context and translation notes. Individual accounts will be presented as individual accounts, not as representative of all affected people.

Looking ahead

From agencies to agency.

“From Agencies to Agency” is Tom Fletcher's proposed title for a future chapter of humanitarian and development cooperation, in conversation with UNDP (opens in a new tab). It is not an IASC programme title.

The purpose of reform is not simply to reorganize institutions. It is to strengthen the agency of people and communities, improve the way organizations work together, and connect immediate relief with recovery, preparedness and longer-term development.

From institutional commitments to collective outcomes: a humanitarian system that is not merely reorganized, but demonstrably more capable of serving the people affected by crises.

Proposed editorial copy for this page; not an IASC quotation or adopted slogan.
