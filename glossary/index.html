Glossary

The words that recur across Cadre 22, Cadre 7 and Cadre 509 — what a grade means, what counts as a second source, and what a cell's status is actually saying. This page describes what the three desks already do; it isn't a new rule.

SourcingEvidence grades

Every source cited by any of the three desks is graded on how direct it is to the event or document it reports.

AGrade A
Official or primary — a government body, a UN mission or Security Council document, a court or legislative record, a signed statement.
BGrade B
Established press or a recognised monitor — a wire service or newspaper with its own reporting, not simply relaying someone else's release.
CGrade C
Not yet graded. Listed for the record, but never counted toward the two-source threshold below — a claim resting only on Grade C material is treated as unsourced.

The core ruleIndependent originators

The desks count originators — who actually produced the information — not documents or outlets. A story and the wire report it was built on are one originator, however many places reprint it.

"Two independent originators or empty."
"A republication of the same report is not corroboration; one originator through two channels counts once."

A cell or claim with one qualifying originator, or none, stays open — it is never filled by inference, and a single strong source is not treated as settling it.

What you're looking atCell and claim states

Empty
No qualifying source yet found. This is itself a reported result, not a gap waiting to be filled in later.
Candidate
Two independent originators are on record, but the desk hasn't yet signed a reading — shown, but marked unsigned until it is.
Held / signed
The desk has signed a judgement. Every judgement on these pages is marked signed or unsigned; nothing is presented as settled by default.
Struck
A later correction to something already signed. Struck entries are kept and shown, with the reason, rather than quietly removed.

How sure, not just how sourcedConfidence

Cadre 22 marks each signed judgement with a three-step confidence bar — low, moderate, high — read from how the evidence actually lines up, not from how firmly the desk wants to state it. Cadre 509 follows the same principle on its own quarterly cells: a confidence level, once declared, is never raised after the fact to make a claim sound stronger.

Cadre 22 keeps a fuller methodology page of its own — grading, sourcing and the full note format — linked from its Method section.
